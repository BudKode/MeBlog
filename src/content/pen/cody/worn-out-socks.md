I can't believe it has been this long but I wrote this back in 2022. It was delivered orally before that. At the time my family and I lived in Woodbury Minnesota and it was at least a 25 minute drive to anywhere we wanted to go except for a target or a lake.

Our first child would get upset sitting in the car for so long (this was back before we just gave our kids an iPad whenever they wanted one) and so we came up with a creative and interactive way to entertain ourselves on the long drives. We would each take a turn coming up with a story on the spot. My son had probably one coherent one the whole time we lived there but he was two and so I was impressed. I had a few that I was actually pretty proud of but don't remember many. This is one that I took the time to write.

I hope you enjoy it.

_Stories from the Road Worn Out Socks_

## Fergus’ Darned Socks

_By Tanner Cranor_

There was an old man named Fergus McKiltan\
who often made fun of new trends.

When those that he knew\
would buy something new\
he would mock and would laugh at his friends.

He mended old clothes and held old traditions\
and both of these served him quite well.

For near five times a day\
came a salesman his way\
with poorly made products to sell.

He was proud of rough sleeves\
and his old, worn-out knees\
but he was the most proud of his socks.

Though they had grown thin\
He still darned and he pinned\
until one day he ran out of luck.

With no fabric to spare\
he was forced to go where\
the salesman resided all day.

Once he stepped inside\
he wanted to hide\
but bare piggies convinced him to stay.

“Welcome!” said one,\
whose name tag said Tom,\
“what brings you in today?”

“New shoes?” Asked the man.\
Fergus raised his hand,\
“it’s just socks for me, I’m afraid.”

“Right this way!” The man beamed,\
“we’ve got options indeed,\
and a sale on titanium weave!”

Though truly intrigued\
Fergus intervened,\
“There is only one sock that I need.”

Fergus held up his sock.\
There wasn’t a lot,\
but Tom knew it without drawing near.

His smile hung down\
and he said with a frown,\
“I’m afraid we don’t sell that sock here.”

Fergus was aghast,\
“Well, why not?!” he asked,\
his poor sock held clenched in his hand.

“It’s just that-”, Tom began.\
“Never mind!” Fergus spat,\
“just show me your most dull and bland”

They wandered down aisles of grays, browns, and blacks.\
Tom did his best to stay strong.

But with each pair he despaired\
as Fergus would glare\
and then yell aloud, “this is wrong!”

“I should have known better!” the grump shouted at last,\
“than to trust such a peddler as you!”

“You think you’ve got a selection of socks?” Fergus bawled,\
“We’ll see what my computer can do!”

So Fergus stormed home to boot up his computer\
and hunt on the world wide web.

But the landscape had changed!\
Flashing ads screamed his name!\
He pushed on through confusion and dread

When at last he succeeded\
he bought more than he needed,\
at 14.99 for each pair.

“No more darning for me!”\
he said, slapping his knee.\
He knew that he’d have socks to spare.

When the new socks arrived,\
he wore two at a time!\
No stiches to bother his toes.

“What’s more,” he declared,\
“I’ll see no more sock sales!”\
Then he felt a sock rip and he froze.

His shock soon abandoned,\
he knew what had happened.\
It must be his old worn out shoes!

Rough, worn, and battered,\
and with snags in the leather,\
they’d torn both his new socks right through.

With face fallen, defeated,\
Thoughts on salesmen, mistreated.\
He felt sorry for what he had done.

He’d try one new pair of shoes\
then tell Tom the news\
and the source of the socks that he’d won.

The next trip to the mall\
did not go that way at all.\
Tom had some unfortunate news.

“Your shoes look just fine,\
I wish you’d stayed last time.\
There was something I wanted to tell you”

“We once sold those socks\
but threw them out, by the box.\
Shoppers complained who had bought them.

They ought to have been thicker.\
They wear out much quicker\
than even our cheapest of options.”

Fergus thought of years huddled over those socks.\
Time he could have been enjoying.

If he hadn’t returned\
he would never have learned\
spending years more with them, toiling.

Fergus knew he’d learned a lesson that day.\
He’d no longer treat salesman poorly.

He apologized\
and the look in his eyes\
told Tom that he truly was sorry.

Fergus still mends\
but he watches the trends\
and thinks twice before making decisions.

He checks what he knows,\
before patching old holes,\
and what he does buy make for great new traditions.
