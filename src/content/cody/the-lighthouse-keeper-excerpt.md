The lamp had been dark for eleven years, which was why Marlow noticed when it blinked.

Once. Twice. Then a long, patient steadiness, like an eye opening in a room where everyone swore the sleeper was gone.

He set down his tea and listened. The wind hadn't changed. The sea still dragged its gravel over the rocks below. But the tower above him hummed, low and warm, the way a house hums when someone has come home.

"Nobody's up there," he said aloud, because saying it made it true.

The stairs creaked once, from the top.
